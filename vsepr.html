Introduction

VSEPR theory, or Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion theory, is used to predict the three-dimensional shapes of molecules by considering that electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible. This includes both bonding pairs, which are shared electrons forming single, double, or triple bonds, and lone pairs, which are unshared electrons on the central atom. By counting these electron domains, VSEPR theory can determine the electron geometry of a molecule, which may differ from its molecular geometry if lone pairs are present. For example, molecules with two, three, four, five, or six electron domains typically have a linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal, or octahedral electron geometry, respectively, with molecular shapes adjusted depending on the number of lone pairs.
