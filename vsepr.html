Introduction

From basic Chemistry, we know that atoms bond with each other to form molecules, but have you ever wondered what the shapes of these molecules are? Can’t atoms just bond in infinitely many different orientations?

To answer this question, we use VSEPR Theory.

VSEPR Theory, or Valence Shell Electron-Pair Repulsion Theory, allows us to predict the three-dimensional structure of molecules by examining the number of bonds and lone electron pairs in their Lewis structures.

Core Idea of VSEPR Theory

Electrons repel each other.

Because electrons carry the same negative charge, regions of high electron density around a central atom push away from one another. To minimize this repulsion, these regions arrange themselves as far apart as possible in three-dimensional space.

Types of Electron Pairs

Electrons in the valence shell may exist as:

  • Bonding pairs – electrons shared between atoms
  • Lone pairs – electrons localized on the central atom

Regions of Electron Density

Each of the following counts as one region:

  • Single, double, or triple bond
  • Lone pair
