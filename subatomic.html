Subatomic Particles

Introduction

All matter is made of atoms, and atoms themselves are composed of even smaller particles called subatomic particles. The three main subatomic particles are protons, neutrons, and electrons. Each of these affects the structure, behavior, and properties of matter. Subatomic particles explain many key concepts in chemistry:

  • Chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of electrons
  • Ions form when atoms gain or lose electrons
  • Isotopes affect atomic mass and nuclear stability
  • Bonding and reactivity depend on valence electrons

Protons

Protons are positively charged particles found in the nucleus of the atom. The number of protons, called the atomic number, uniquely identifies an element. For example, carbon has 6 protons and oxygen has 8. Changing the number of protons changes the element itself.

Neutrons

Neutrons have no electric charge and are also located in the nucleus. They add mass to the atom and help stabilize the nucleus. Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons are called isotopes. Some isotopes are stable, while others are radioactive.

Electrons

Electrons are negatively charged particles that occupy energy levels around the nucleus. Although they have very little mass, they strongly influence chemical behavior. The arrangement of electrons determines bonding and chemical reactions.

Relative Mass and Charge

Protons and neutrons each have a relative mass of about 1 amu, while electrons have a much smaller mass (about 1/1836 of a proton). Protons carry a +1 charge, electrons carry a −1 charge, and neutrons have no charge.
